# Vision Vault - Comprehensive Department Research Findings

## Research Summary
This document synthesizes research findings for ALL film production departments to inform the Vision Vault knowledge module structure.

---

## 1. DIRECTING DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Overall creative vision and storytelling
- Performance direction and blocking
- Camera placement and visual composition
- Script interpretation and adaptation
- Collaboration with all department heads

### Workflow Phases
**Pre-Production:**
- Multiple script reads (story, emotion, technical, visual, style)
- Storyboarding and shot-listing
- Casting and rehearsals
- Location scouting
- Department head meetings
- Visual style development with DP and PD

**Production:**
- On-set direction of actors and crew
- Blocking and staging
- Performance notes
- Dailies review
- Script adjustments

**Post-Production:**
- Editorial supervision
- Director's cut
- Sound design collaboration
- Music spotting session
- Color grading input

### Key Knowledge Areas (from reference documents)
- Story structure (Aristotle's Poetics, 3-act structure, Hero's Journey)
- Character development and arc
- Thematic depth and symbolism
- Visual storytelling principles
- Collaboration and leadership
- Problem-solving and adaptation
- Audience engagement strategies

## 2. ASSISTANT DIRECTING DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Production scheduling and logistics
- On-set coordination and efficiency
- Safety oversight
- Call sheet creation
- Set management
- Communication hub between director and crew

### Workflow Breakdown

**1st AD Responsibilities:**
- Script breakdown and analysis
- Creating shooting schedule
- Coordinating with all departments
- Managing the "now" - current day's shooting
- Calling the roll (Picture's up, Rolling, Action calls)
- Safety compliance

**2nd AD Responsibilities:**
- Managing the "next" - preparing for upcoming shoots
- Call sheet creation (with 1st AD approval)
- Actor coordination and paperwork
- Background talent management
- Set security and crowd control

**3rd AD Responsibilities:**
- Background action coordination
- Set runner management
- Lower-level logistics support

### Critical Skills
- Meticulous organization
- Clear communication
- Problem-solving under pressure
- Script analysis
- Diplomacy and leadership

## 3. UNIT PRODUCTION MANAGER (UPM) / LINE PRODUCER

### Role Distinction
- **Line Producer**: Creates the budget, overall production oversight
- **UPM**: Executes the budget, day-to-day operations (reports to Line Producer)
- On smaller projects, can be same person

### Core Responsibilities

**Pre-Production:**
- Script breakdown for budgeting
- Preliminary shooting schedule
- Budget preparation and coordination
- Location scouting oversight
- Crew hiring and contracts
- Equipment procurement
- Insurance and legal setup
- Production office establishment

**Production:**
- Daily budget monitoring
- Coordination with all department heads
- Problem resolution within budget constraints
- Production accounting oversight
- Payroll management (critical - unions can shut down for payment issues)
- Schedule feasibility analysis with 1st AD

**Post-Production:**
- Wrap logistics
- Final budget reconciliation
- Asset management

### Work Environment
- Typically 80-100+ hours per week during production
- High-pressure decision making
- Constant communication across all departments

## 4. CINEMATOGRAPHY DEPARTMENT

### Core Knowledge (from reference invocation documents)

**Core Visual Components:**
1. **Space** - Use of frame space, negative space, composition
2. **Line** - Leading lines, horizon lines, vertical/horizontal dynamics
3. **Shape** - Geometric composition, framing within frames
4. **Movement** - Camera movement, subject movement, dynamic composition
5. **Tone** - Contrast ratios, exposure, mood through light/dark
6. **Color** - Color theory, palette selection, emotional impact
7. **Rhythm** - Pacing through camera movement, editing consideration

**Technical Foundations:**
- **Camera**: Sensor size, resolution, frame rates, compression
- **Continuity**: Screen direction, eye-line, action matching
- **Composition**: Rule of thirds, golden ratio, depth
- **Coverage**: Master shots, close-ups, inserts, establishing shots

**Storytelling Elements:**
- Visual metaphor and symbolism
- Character perspective (objective vs subjective camera)
- Emotional beats through lighting and framing
- Subtext visualization

**Practical Considerations:**
- Script analysis and shot planning
- Lookbook and visual references
- Camera and lens testing
- Lighting design
- Collaboration with director, gaffer, key grip, production design
- On-set problem solving

### Workflow Process (from DP Process document)
1. Multiple script reads
2. Lookbook creation
3. Testing (camera, lenses, lighting)
4. Crew selection
5. Pre-production meetings
6. On-set execution (blocking, lighting, photography, administrative)
7. Dailies review
8. Post-production involvement (DI, color grading)

## 5. LIGHTING DEPARTMENT (GAFFER)

### Core Responsibilities
- Execute lighting plan from cinematographer
- Manage electrical team
- Light placement, intensity, and quality control
- Power distribution planning
- Equipment procurement and maintenance

### Workflow and Collaboration
- Works in close partnership with DP and Key Grip
- Handles all **active light sources** (placement, intensity)
- Coordinates with Key Grip on **passive light control** (diffusion, blocking, shaping)
- Technical problem-solving for complex setups

### Required Knowledge
- Lighting ratios and exposure
- Color temperature and CRI
- Electrical systems and safety
- Lighting equipment (LED, HMI, Tungsten)
- Lighting effects (practical integration, motivated lighting)

---

## 6. GRIP DEPARTMENT (KEY GRIP)

### Core Responsibilities
- Camera support and movement systems
- Passive light control (flags, silks, scrims, butterflies)
- Set safety and rigging
- Collaboration with cinematographer and gaffer

### Workflow and Collaboration
- Direct collaboration with DP to translate photographic vision into practical support
- Works side-by-side with Gaffer on lighting setups
- Key Grip handles diffusion, softening, blocking of light
- Gaffer handles light source placement and intensity
- Both coordinate on achieving precise visual effects

### Required Knowledge
- Camera movement systems (dolly, crane, Steadicam, technocrane)
- Rigging and safety
- Grip equipment (C-stands, flags, frames, diffusion)
- Physics of light control
- Problem-solving for complex camera moves

- Character wardrobe design and implementation

- Collaboration with production design for color and style consistency

- Collaboration with hair & makeup
- Managing wardrobe team

**Tools and Documentation:**

## 9. SOUND DEPARTMENT (PRODUCTION)

### Role Structure
- **Production Sound Mixer**: Team leader, responsible for all on-set audio recording
- **Boom Operator**: Microphone placement and operation (reports to mixer)
- **Sound Utility/Assistant**: Equipment support

### Production Sound Mixer Responsibilities

**Pre-Production:**
- Location sound surveys (ambient noise assessment)
- Meeting with producer and director on sound approach
- Equipment selection and testing
- Problem identification and solutions

**Production:**
- Recording high-quality audio for every take
- Microphone selection and placement strategy
- Audio level monitoring and quality control
- Detailed note-taking
- Collaboration with director on take acceptability
- Working with boom operator and sound assistant

### Boom Operator Responsibilities
- Microphone placement (boom pole operation)
- Keeping mic as close as possible without entering frame
- Assisting mixer with wireless mic placement on actors
- Equipment maintenance and repairs
- Script familiarity (must know who speaks when)
- Coordinating mic movement with actor movement and dialogue

### Required Skills
- Script memorization
- High level of focus and concentration
- Physical stamina (holding boom for long takes)
- Diplomacy and people skills
- Technical audio knowledge
- Quick problem-solving

### Collaboration
- Close partnership between mixer and boom operator
- Communication with camera department (framing awareness)
- On lower budgets, roles may be combined into "sound recordist"
- Career path: sound utility → boom operator → production sound mixer

## 10. EDITING DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Assembly of dailies during production
- Rough cut creation
- Fine cut with director collaboration
- Picture lock achievement
- Collaboration with sound, music, VFX, color

### Workflow Stages

**1. Ingesting and Logging:**
- Assistant editors organize and manage footage
- Syncing audio and video
- Labeling and timeline organization
- Creating specific stringouts of scenes

**2. Assembly Edit:**
- Initial assembly based on script
- Organizing dailies in script order

**3. Rough Cut:**
- First creative pass
- Scene structure and pacing

**4. Fine Cut:**
- Highly collaborative process
- Director, producers, studio input
- Refinement of pacing and story

**5. Titling:**
- Opening and closing credits
- Required titles and text

**6. Picture Lock:**
- **Critical milestone** - no more editorial changes
- Required before sound design, music composition, color can finalize
- Any frame change after lock requires resyncing all elements
- Causes issues and extra costs if broken

**7. Sound Track:**
- Working with sound team on final mix

### Post-Picture Lock Process
- Sound design
- Color correction and online
- Visual effects integration
- Final mix
- Deliverables creation

### Key Collaborations
- Close work with director throughout
- Assistant editors handle technical tasks so editors can focus on storytelling
- Sound designer and composer need picture-locked version to begin final work

## 11. VISUAL EFFECTS (VFX) DEPARTMENT

### VFX Supervisor Role

**Core Responsibilities:**
- Artistic direction and technical execution of VFX sequences
- Managing VFX pipeline and team
- Ultimate responsibility for all VFX elements

**On-Set Responsibilities (2025):**
- Advising director and DP on shooting for VFX integration
- Monitoring every production stage
- Ensuring desired result feasibility
- Lidar supervisor on set (3D volumetric scans of sets/locations)
- Photography and surveying of sets

**Post-Production:**
- Managing global team of VFX artists
- Overseeing modeling, animation, simulation, texturing, lighting, compositing
- Working with Technical Directors on custom tools and plugins
- Pipeline efficiency optimization

### VFX Pipeline (2025 Workflow)
- Emphasis on global collaboration
- Cloud-based workflows
- Seamless integration across all production phases
- Real-time collaboration even with distributed teams

### Collaboration
- Works with director and DP during prep and production
- Coordinates with production design for visual consistency
- Integrates with editorial team
- Collaborates with color grading

## 12. STUNT COORDINATION DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Coordinating, choreographing, and casting all stunts
- **Primary focus: safety of all cast and crew**
- Planning stunt sequences from beginning to end

### Workflow Process

**Pre-Production:**
- Script analysis and stunt identification
- Collaboration with director on vision
- Storyboarding stunt sequences frame-by-frame
- Developing rehearsal plans
- Casting stunt performers
- Risk assessment and safety planning

**Production:**
- Constant communication with director, AD, camera operator
- Ensuring stunts read on camera
- Creating environment for open dialogue on safety
- Using proper safety equipment (harnesses, padding, crash mats)
- On-set supervision of all stunt execution

### Fight Choreography (Fight Coordinator)
- Unlike stunt coordinators (who handle all action), fight coordinators focus exclusively on fights
- Breaking down fight choreography into manageable segments
- Working with director and actors on specific moves, tone, pacing
- Assisting in script breakdowns for fight sequences

### 2025 Industry Developments
- **New Oscar category**: Achievement in Stunt Design (debuts at 100th Oscars in 2028 for 2027 films)
- **Pre-visualization technology**: Used in productions like Cobra Kai to refine fight choreography for enhanced safety and precision

### Required Knowledge
- Extensive health and safety code knowledge
- Choreography and performance skills
- Technical rigging expertise
- Communication and leadership

## 13. LOCATION MANAGEMENT DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Finding and securing filming locations
- Balancing creative vision with practical logistics, budget, legal considerations
- Managing all aspects of location usage

### Workflow Process

**1. Script Breakdown:**
- Determine every location needed from script

**2. Location Scouting (3-4 visits per location typically):**
- Initial scouting and photography
- Narrowing down options
- Detailed documentation (photos, video for light/sound)
- Reporting to director and production designer

**3. Clearing the Location:**
- Investigating availability
- Negotiating contracts and rates with location owners
- Obtaining certificate of insurance
- **Obtaining film permits** from local authorities and police (may involve fees)
- Distributing "resident letters" or "filming notifications" to neighbors

**4. Production Support:**
- Managing current set (assistant location manager)
- Prepping next location (location manager)
- Dealing with pedestrians, neighbors, permit checks
- Coordinating parking, base camp, crew access

**5. Wrap and Restoration:**
- Location department is last to depart
- **Leaving location in same or better condition than arrival**

### Required Skills
- Negotiation and diplomacy
- Legal and permit knowledge
- Budget management
- Problem-solving
- Photography and documentation
- Coordination and logistics

## 14. CASTING DEPARTMENT

### Core Responsibilities
- Finding and selecting actors for all roles
- Managing audition process
- Balancing creative vision with practical considerations

### Workflow Timeline
- **Studio features**: 9 months to 1 year ahead of shooting
- **Indie films**: Typically 6 weeks prior to shoot (varies from 5 days to 9 months based on needs/budget)

### Process Breakdown

**1. Receiving Specifications:**
- Character age ranges
- Ethnicities
- Required skills
- Other specific requirements from director/producers

**2. Breakdown Distribution:**
- Posting to casting sites (like Backstage.com)
- Sending directly to talent agents
- Open calls for lesser-known actors

**3. Submissions Review:**
- Reviewing many submissions from agents and actors
- Scheduling known actors
- Giving opportunities to new talent
- Requiring video reels (résumés alone insufficient)

**4. Audition Process:**
- Providing "sides" (dialogue samples for audition)
- **Two types**: In-person and self-tape
- **Pre-reads**: Audition only for casting director
- **Callbacks**: Successful candidates audition for director/producers

**5. Final Selection:**
- Director/producer collaboration
- Negotiations and contracts

### Industry Tools (2025)
- Online casting platforms
- Self-tape submissions
- Virtual auditions
- Digital portfolio management

## 15. MUSIC DEPARTMENT

### Role Structure
- **Composer**: Creates original score
- **Music Supervisor**: Curates overall musical sound, manages existing music licensing

### Composer Workflow

**Spotting Session:**
- Meeting between director and composer
- Watching rough cut together
- Discussing where music should appear
- Determining what music should accomplish emotionally
- Can be in-person or virtual (2025 practice)

**Composition Process:**
- Creating themes and motifs
- Scoring to picture lock
- Recording sessions
- Mix notes and revisions

### Music Supervisor Workflow

**Core Responsibilities:**
- Overseeing all musical aspects of production
- Collaborating with creator/director on musical sound
- Determining which moments call for music
- Working closely with composer on original music
- Producing and managing on-camera musical moments
- **Negotiating and licensing all music**
- Managing music budget
- Handling spotting sessions, file delivery, mix notes

**Collaboration:**
- Acts as middle person between director and composer
- Works with composer to determine which scenes need original score vs pre-existing music
- Coordinates with post-production sound team

### 2025 Practices
- Virtual spotting sessions common
- Ongoing communication encouraged beyond initial spotting
- Close coordination with composer throughout process

## CROSS-DEPARTMENTAL THEMES

### Common Elements Across All Departments
1. **Script Analysis**: Every department starts with deep script study
2. **Collaboration**: Constant communication with director and other departments
3. **Pre-Production Preparation**: Most critical work happens before cameras roll
4. **Budget Awareness**: All departments must work within allocated resources
5. **Problem-Solving**: Adapting to changing circumstances
6. **Documentation**: Detailed note-taking and tracking
7. **Safety**: Paramount concern across all physical production
8. **Technology Integration**: 2025 workflows increasingly cloud-based and globally collaborative

### Department Interdependencies

**Visual Triangle:**
- Directing ↔ Cinematography ↔ Production Design
- Must align on color, composition, mood, visual style

**Physical Production:**
- UPM ↔ 1st AD ↔ Location
- Coordinate schedule, logistics, feasibility

**Lighting Partnership:**
- Cinematography ↔ Gaffer ↔ Key Grip
- DP plans, Gaffer executes active light, Grip controls passive light

**Character Visualization:**
- Directing ↔ Costume ↔ Hair & Makeup ↔ Production Design
- Unified approach to character appearance and evolution

**Post-Production Chain:**
- Editing → Picture Lock → (VFX, Sound, Music, Color in parallel) → Final Mix

### Production Phase Intersections

**Development → Pre-Production:**
- Writing, Directing, Producing, Production Design begin early

**Pre-Production → Production:**
- All departments culminate in shooting

**Production → Post-Production:**
- Editing begins during production (dailies)
- VFX supervision on set for post work
- Sound recording for post sound design

**Post-Production → Distribution:**
- Marketing begins during post
- Deliverables prepared for distribution
